Rules

This page describes the default rules. Tariff, number of hands, Tout, card deck (Bavarian or French), starting dealer and time per turn can be changed in the app.

Goal

Four players use 32 cards worth 120 card points in total. In every game one player (or two in the called-ace game) plays against the others and wins with at least 61 card points. A match lasts 12 games; whoever has the most points afterwards wins. Scoring is in points, not money.

Cards and card points

Ace 11, Ten 10, King 4, Ober 3, Unter 2; Nine, Eight and Seven count nothing. Ace 11, Ten 10, King 4, Queen 3, Jack 2; Nine, Eight and Seven count nothing.

Trumps

In the called-ace game and Solo all Obers, all Unters and the cards of the trump suit (Hearts in the called-ace game) are trump. Order: Obers and Unters, each in the order Acorns, Leaves, Hearts, Bells, then Ace, Ten, King, Nine, Eight, Seven of the trump suit. In Wenz only the four Unters are trump; in the suits the order is Ace, Ten, King, Ober, Nine, Eight, Seven. In the called-ace game and Solo all Queens, all Jacks and the cards of the trump suit (Hearts in the called-ace game) are trump. Order: Queens and Jacks, each in the order Clubs, Spades, Hearts, Diamonds, then Ace, Ten, King, Nine, Eight, Seven of the trump suit. In Wenz only the four Jacks are trump; in the suits the order is Ace, Ten, King, Queen, Nine, Eight, Seven.

Bidding

Starting left of the dealer, everyone passes or announces a game. A bid must beat the previous one: called ace, then Wenz and Solo (equal rank; the player closer to the lead wins), then Tout. If everyone passes, the cards are dealt again.

Called-ace game

The player calls the ace of a suit other than Hearts. They need at least one plain card of that suit (no Ober or Unter) and must not hold the ace. Whoever holds the called ace is the partner; this only becomes clear when it is played. When the called suit is led, the holder must play the ace. The holder may lead the suit only with the ace itself and may not discard it while the suit has not been led; in the last trick it is the only card left. With the ace and at least three more cards of the suit, the holder may “run away” and lead another card of that suit.

Wenz, Solo and Tout

In Wenz only the Unters are trump; in Solo the player chooses the trump suit. They play alone against the other three. In Tout the player must win all eight tricks.

Play

The player left of the dealer leads. Players must follow suit, or play trump if trump was led; a player who cannot follow plays any card. There is no obligation to win the trick. The highest trump wins the trick or, without trump, the highest card of the suit led.

Scoring

The called-ace game counts the base rate of 10 points; Wenz, Solo and Tout count the solo rate of 20 points. One extra rate is added for Schneider (the winner has at least 91 card points), for Schwarz (the winner takes all tricks) and for each runner. Runners are the highest trumps in a row held by one side (Obers Acorns, Leaves, Hearts, Bells, then Unters; in Wenz only Unters), from three, in Wenz from two. Tout counts double, without Schneider and Schwarz. In the called-ace game each winner receives the value from one loser; in Solo each opponent pays the player the value (or the other way round).

Time per turn

You have 60 seconds per turn. When time runs out, “Pass” is said for you, the first allowed suit is chosen or an allowed card is played.

In the app

Tap a card to play it. Only allowed cards are highlighted. You see the points of each game after the last trick.

Several humans

If several humans share the device, hands are hidden after every move. Pass the device on; the next player taps the screen.

Computer

Computer players only know their own cards and what lies open on the table.
